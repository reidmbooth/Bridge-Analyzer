Selected board interpretation

    How flags work:
    DD the player took fewer tricks as declarer than the double-dummy solver says were makeable from that exact seat, in that exact suit, on that board.
    PEER another pair who declared the same suit from the same seat on that board (any contract level) took more tricks than the player did.
    AVG the player took fewer tricks than the average of other pairs who played the same contract from the same seat on that board.
    BID-DD the partnership chose the double-dummy best suit but bid above the double-dummy makeable level.
    BID-AVG the partnership chose a suit but bid above the average level played in that suit from the same seat.
    ATYPICAL the partnership's suit was not among the most commonly played suits from that seat on the board.
    DEF-DD while defending, the partnership allowed more tricks than the double-dummy analysis says the declarer should make.
    DEF-AVG while defending, the partnership allowed more tricks than the field average for that contract and declaring seat.
    BOTTOM the partnership had the worst comparable result: fewest tricks when declaring or most tricks allowed when defending.
    LOW-DD the partnership stopped below a game or slam level supported by double dummy.
    LOW-AVG the partnership stopped below a game or slam level supported by the field's average bid.
    BAD-SUIT the partnership reached game or slam in a suit that was worse than both the double-dummy and field-preferred suits.
    DOUBLE while defending, double dummy says the undoubled contract should fail, so the partnership could have doubled it.
    Rows are sorted with the most-flagged boards first. Comparisons are always seat-for-seat (e.g. North-declared spades vs. North-declared spades) since North/South/East/West each hold a fixed, different hand for a given board — only same-seat results are truly apples-to-apples.
